# Research Brief: Momentum After Turnover and Trading Costs

## Executive conclusion

A historical 2006-2022 experiment on a seven-stock technology universe produced a **22.5% net CAGR and 0.93 Sharpe ratio**, compared with **17.2% CAGR and 0.88 Sharpe** for an equal-weight benchmark. The strategy deducted 10-basis-point turnover costs and used signals lagged by one week.

The result is economically interesting but not sufficient for allocation. The universe is small, concentrated, and survivorship-biased. It should be read as evidence that the implementation works and that momentum warrants broader testing, not as a forecast of future performance.

## Portfolio construction

- Weekly observations and rebalancing
- Cross-sectional momentum over 4-, 12-, 26-, and 52-week horizons
- One-week signal lag to avoid look-ahead bias
- Equal weights across the top-ranked securities
- One-way turnover and proportional transaction costs
- Equal-weight universe benchmark

## Client implication

Before using the signal in a client portfolio, expand the universe, evaluate sector-neutral rankings, test multiple cost assumptions, use point-in-time constituents, and examine performance by inflation, growth, and rate regime. Allocation should depend on robustness, diversification contribution, and drawdown behavior—not headline CAGR alone.

## Limitations

The reference figures come from a narrow technology universe. Market impact, capacity, taxes, borrow constraints, and delistings are not fully modeled. Results are research evidence, not investment advice.
